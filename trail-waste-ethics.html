What Goes In Must Come Out: Trail Waste Ethics

Nature has no bins - what you carry in, you must carry out.

Leave the Trail Better Than You Found It

When we venture into the wild, nature offers us peace, clarity, and beauty. But with that gift comes responsibility. Whether you are hiking, camping, or simply enjoying a forest trail, everything you bring with you has an impact. Trail waste ethics are a promise to leave no trace and to protect the places that make us feel most alive.

What Is Trail Waste?

Trail waste includes anything left behind on a hike - plastic wrappers, bottles, food scraps, and even tiny items like tissues or bottle caps. Each piece of litter disrupts wildlife, pollutes soil, and degrades the experience for everyone who follows.

Why It Matters?

In natural environments, waste does not disappear. Even "biodegradable" items like orange peels or tissues can take months to break down and can attract wildlife. Human waste, if not handled properly, spreads disease and alters soil chemistry. Leaving waste behind disrespects the very environment we seek to enjoy.

Dealing With Human Waste

  • Use designated toilets whenever possible.
  • In remote areas, bury waste 6-8 inches deep and at least 200 feet from water sources or trails.
  • Pack out used toilet paper and hygiene products in sealed bags.
  • On sensitive trails, use a portable toilet bag and pack it out.

What About Food Waste?

  • Do not feed wildlife - even accidentally.
  • Pack out all food scraps, including biodegradable ones.
  • Use a trash bag or snack pouch to store waste until proper disposal.

EcoTrails Tip: Carry a Waste Kit

  • Ziplock or dry bag for trash
  • Small trowel for catholes
  • Biodegradable soap and sanitizer
  • Toilet paper in a waterproof pouch
  • Extra bag for hygiene waste

The Golden Rule: Pack It In, Pack It Out

Whatever you bring to the trail, take it back with you - no exceptions. This includes food wrappers, bottles, wipes, and even organic scraps. Be the hiker who leaves the trail cleaner than they found it. If we all do our part, the wild stays wild.
